Effect of a Phenotype-Tailored Lifestyle Intervention on Caloric Intake and Macronutrient Composition in Adults with Obesity: A Secondary Analysis of the PHENO-Diet Trial.

A 12-week trial demonstrated that phenotype-tailored lifestyle intervention (PLI) was associated with greater weight loss than standard lifestyle intervention (SLI); however, differences in dietary patterns between groups were not examined. This secondary analysis evaluated caloric and macronutrient intake at 12 weeks in adults with obesity (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m Groups were compared at 12 weeks adjusting for baseline values. PLI was associated with lower total energy intake than SLI (1273 ± 182 vs. 1894 ± 176 kcal/day; PLI was associated with lower energy intake, mainly due to reduced carbohydrate and fat intake, and a higher relative contribution of protein, consistent with phenotype-based recommendations rather than macronutrient prescriptions. These findings suggest a potential role for personalized dietary approaches in obesity management. However, the non-randomized design does not allow causal inferences.
