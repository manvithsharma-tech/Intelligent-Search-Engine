Safety, Tolerability and Antiviral Activity of GST-HG131 in Chronic Hepatitis B: A Randomized, Double-Blind Phase IIa Study.

Chronic hepatitis B (CHB) remains a major global health burden. Current therapies rarely achieve a functional cure due to low rates of HBsAg clearance, the persistence of the viral replication template-covalently closed circular DNA (cccDNA)-and the need for lifelong treatment. GST-HG131 is a first-in-class, orally bioavailable small-molecule inhibitor of PAPD5/7, key host factors involved in HBV mRNA stabilization and HBsAg production. We evaluated the safety, tolerability and antiviral activity of GST-HG131 in patients with CHB receiving nucleos(t)ide analogue (NUC) therapy in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase IIa study. This double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled Phase IIa study evaluated the efficacy and safety of GST-HG131 in 45 CHB patients. Cohorts 1 and 2 (n = 10 each; 8 active, 2 placebo) received 30 mg or 60 mg BID for 28 days, respectively. Cohort 3 (n = 25; 20 active, five placebo) received 30 mg BID for 12 weeks. All participants were on nucleos(t)ide analog monotherapy for more than 6 months, with HBsAg between 100 and 1500 IU/mL, and serum ALT below 1× ULN. The greatest antiviral activity was observed after 12 weeks of treatment, supporting further clinical development of GST-HG131 as a component of functional cure strategies for CHB. ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT06263959. Chronic hepatitis B (CHB) remains a major global health challenge because current antiviral therapies rarely achieve a functional cure, mainly due to the persistence of hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) and stable viral templates in infected liver cells. This study evaluated GST‐HG131, an oral PAPD5/7 inhibitor, in patients with CHB receiving stable nucleos(t)ide analogue therapy. Treatment with GST‐HG131 resulted in significant reductions in HBsAg levels, with the greatest effect observed after 12 weeks of treatment. The treatment was safe and generally well tolerated. These findings suggest that GST‐HG131 may represent a promising therapeutic approach to enhance functional cure rates for patients with CHB.
